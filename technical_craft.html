Built natively. Composed deliberately.

Technical Craft

For those curious about the work beneath the calm, this is how Inner Thought is made: with native Apple frameworks, clear boundaries, recoverable states, and care for the whole person using it.

Native by design

Inner Thought is written in Swift and SwiftUI, using SwiftData, AVFoundation, Speech, MapKit, PhotosUI, WidgetKit, ActivityKit, App Intents, CryptoKit, and LocalAuthentication. Apple-native frameworks keep the app close to the platform, private by default, and light on dependencies.

Small, focused responsibilities

The interface expresses state; focused controllers and services own recording, transcription, storage, playback, media, location, encryption, and system presentation. Shared coordinators connect those pieces without making a screen responsible for everything.

The thought comes first

Audio is authoritative and its destination exists before capture begins. Pauses, interruptions, transcription failures, and storage errors are designed as recoverable states. A valid recording is never discarded because speech recognition failed.

Private by structure

Titles, transcripts, locations, custom tags, audio, photos, and videos are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key lives in the iOS Keychain; decrypted working files use platform file protection and are purged when the app leaves the foreground. There are no accounts, ads, trackers, or third-party analytics.

One app, every way in

Semantic controls, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, Voice Control, Switch Control, keyboard access, Reduce Motion, Increase Contrast, and non-colour cues are considered across complete tasks. Accessibility is evaluated as a path someone can finish, not a count of modifiers.

The person comes first

Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines and accessibility principles guide us to put the person—not the feature—at the centre of each decision. Inner Thought considers people with visual, hearing, mobility, speech, and cognitive disabilities, along with temporary and situational limitations. Meaning never depends on colour, motion, sound, or gesture alone; enhancements remain optional; privacy and dignity are preserved. Automated evidence supports this work, and release claims wait for physical assistive-technology verification.

Language in the architecture

Every visible and spoken interface string lives in String Catalogs. Layouts are bidirectional, user-authored words keep their language, and dates, times, and numbers follow the active locale. Fourteen launch languages are built in; draft translations still require fluent human review before release.

Native beyond the app

Home and Lock Screen widgets, Live Activities, the Dynamic Island, and App Intents share a deliberately small command layer and capture coordinator. Private thought text stays out of system surfaces; only the state needed to control an active capture leaves the app interface.

Evidence, not confidence

Deterministic unit tests exercise persistence, encryption, recovery, media, mapping, and capture state. UI tests cover navigation, expanded text, and right-to-left layout. Automated checks support the work; physical iPhone and assistive-technology testing remain release gates.

Engineering in service of calm

Grace was not painted over function at the end. Progressive disclosure, semantic typography, stable layout space, restrained motion, and quiet materials let substantial capability remain calm and understandable.

The result is meant to feel simple because the complexity has been carried carefully underneath.
